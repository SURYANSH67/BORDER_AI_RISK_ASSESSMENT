RESEARCH PROTOTYPE
01

1. INTRODUCTION & BACKGROUND

Border security operations along rugged, mountainous terrain are fundamentally constrained by asymmetric geographical barriers, dynamic micro-climates, dense vegetative concealment corridors, and operational patrol coverage gaps. Traditional surveillance approaches rely either on isolated human observation posts or disjoint sensor networks.

Modern defense intelligence requires a unified, multi-modal decision support framework capable of ingesting diverse modalities—ranging from digital elevation models (DEM) and remote-sensing vegetation indices (NDVI) to meteorological forecasts, operational patrol shift schedules, historical infiltration attempts, and simulated thermal/optical camera triggers and acoustic/seismic sensor signals.

This report documents the architectural design, implementation, and empirical validation of the EMBARIA Border Risk Intelligence System (border_infli_ai), a research-grade prototype validated on the Srinagar Sector border region across 150 spatial zones (SGR_ZONE_001 to SGR_ZONE_150).

Objective: To design and evaluate a hybrid multi-modal ML system that predicts and explains border infiltration risk at a fine spatial granularity and supports actionable operational decisions.
02

2. LITERATURE REVIEW & RELATED WORK

Prior research in automated border surveillance has largely focused on single-domain approaches:

Computer Vision & Aerial UAV Tracking

Applications of YOLOv8 and CNNs for human/vehicle detection along border perimeters.

Geospatial Analysis & Terrain Modeling

GIS-based slope and terrain cost-distance algorithms (e.g., Tobler's hiking function).

Remote Sensing & Vegetation Indices

NDVI, NDWI, and land cover studies for concealment assessment.

Patrol Scheduling & Operations

Optimization models for patrol route planning and resource allocation.

Anomaly Detection & Time-Series

Isolation Forest, LSTM, and statistical methods for intrusion pattern detection.

Research Gap: Limited studies integrate multi-modal environmental, operational, historical, and surveillance data with explainability for actionable border risk intelligence.
03

3. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & MULTI-MODAL DATA FUSION

The architecture unifies seven distinct data modalities into a standardized spatial matrix across 150 zones:

Terrain (519k DEM Pts) ──┐ Weather (Atmospheric) ───┤ Vegetation (NDVI) ───────┤ Patrol Logs (1.8k Shifts)┼───> Spatial Normalization ───> Fused Master Matrix (150 × 48) Historical (5.2k Events) ┤ │ Camera (1.2k Triggers) ──┤ ├───> Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation Sensors (1.5k Readings) ─┘ │ ├───> SHAP TreeExplainer Local/Global Attribution │ └───> Decision Engine ───> Personnel Allocation Index

5. DECISION ENGINE & RELATIVE RESOURCE ALLOCATION INDEX

To maintain scientific defensibility and avoid inventing uncalibrated military troop deployments, the system formulates recommendations using a dataset-derived Personnel Allocation Index ($I_{\text{alloc}}$) relative to a standard 80% patrol coverage baseline:

I_{\text{alloc}} = 1.0 + \left(\frac{\text{Coverage Gap}}{100}\right) \times 1.2 + \left(\frac{\text{Risk Score} - 50}{150}\right)

An index of 1.08× baseline denotes that approximately 8% more patrol-resource coverage is recommended relative to the configured operational baseline during identified high-risk intervals.
